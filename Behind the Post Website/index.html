The Meme-ification of Warfare

Key Quote "Memes function like modern-day propaganda posters—they carry strategic, targeted messages intended to manipulate or mislead users. Though often dismissed as harmless jokes, memes frequently contain coded messages and strategic framing beneath the humor. Repeated exposure to such propagandistic memes can distort reality, normalize aggression, and reinforce power imbalances. As a result, previously unacceptable ideas begin to feel commonplace, which reduces critical scrutiny of their meaning and impact."

Memes & Culture

The Function of Memes in Political Discourse

Glenn Anderau and Daniel Barbarrusa

The use of memes has become increasingly widespread in political discourse. However, there is a dearth of philosophical discussion on memes and their impact on political discourse. This paper addresses this gap in the literature and bridges the divide between the empirical and philosophical work on memes by offering a functionalist account which allows for a more in-depth analysis of the role memes play in political discourse.

Memetic Warfare 2006

In Evolutionary Psychology, Memes and the Origin of War, 2006

Keith Henson

This paper combines evolutionary psychology and memetics to explain war as a species-typical behavior rooted in humanity's Stone Age past. Henson argues that population growth leading to resource scarcity—what he calls "looming privation"—activates an evolved behavioral "switch." This switch increases the spread of xenophobic or warlike memes (cultural ideas, beliefs), which synchronize groups to launch coordinated attacks on neighboring tribes.

Memetics: A Growth Industry in US Military Operations

Michael Prosse

The paper argues that future warfare must be fought in the "ideological spheres" of enemies and the undecided. It advocates for "weaponeering" memes—treating cultural ideas as transmittable units—as a primary tool to win this metaphysical fight, moving beyond traditional Information Operations (IO) and Psychological Operations (PsyOps)

Memes & Culture 2021

Messy on the inside: internet memes as mapping tools of everyday life

Sulafa Zidani

This paper presents the first systematic analysis of memes created by Palestinians in Israel. An analysis of 150 memes reveals how memes are used to reflect on and intervene in Palestinian youths’ navigation of life in mixed cities under prolonged war and colonialism. I analyze these memes at three different levels: global popular culture and politics, in relation to the Israeli State, and Palestinian in-group dynamics.
